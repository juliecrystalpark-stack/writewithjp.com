UX writing · Voice and tone · Content guidelines
Confidential mobile game project

Giving a game team a shared writing reference

A style guide built from scratch to support clearer rules, consistent terminology and the game’s voice.

Explore the case study

The challenge: Game-rule copy varied in clarity and structure. Some instructions were easy to follow, while others needed more direct wording. The team needed consistent guidance it could apply across different features.

My contribution: I created a style guide from scratch, using established guides as references and tailoring the guidance to the game.

For game rules, I established an action-led formula: start with a clear verb and explain what the player needs to do. I also clarified when capitalization was appropriate—for terms that belonged to the game’s established language, rather than for emphasis alone.

The wider guide covered voice and tone, player-focused writing, plain language, brevity, word choice, grammar and mechanics. It included formatting guidance for game rules and monetization copy, alongside accessibility and inclusion.

Why this approach: Editing individual instructions addresses the copy in front of you. A reusable structure gives the team a way to approach the next instruction, too. My aim was to preserve the game’s personality while making its rules clearer and more consistent.

The outcome: The guide was delivered and approved, with positive client feedback. It gave the team a documented reference for writing and reviewing future content.

Tutorial writing · Character voice · Editing
Confidential mobile game project

A new character voice. A clearer tutorial.

Shorter instructions and themed dialogue, with the necessary mechanical information preserved.

Explore the case study

The task: Adapt an existing tutorial to a themed version of the game, matching the narrator’s personality while keeping the mechanics understandable.

My contribution: I rewrote the dialogue around the character and the game’s bright, playful voice. I shortened lengthy text, checked that the necessary mechanical information remained and made instructions more explicit about the action players needed to take.

My thinking: Personality should help carry an instruction. I considered how the character would speak, then checked whether players could still understand what to do. Where a cue described tapping without explaining its purpose, I clarified the relationship between the action and its effect.

Collaboration: I worked with the team lead to review meaning and direction, revising around character limits and terminology the team needed to retain. I also suggested alternative progression wording; the team kept its existing “Tap to continue” prompt.

The outcome: The team retained my rewritten tutorial, including the shorter dialogue and themed character voice.

Localization editing · Naming · Content consistency
Confidential mobile game project

Making recurring promotions easier to recognize

A consistent approach to naming recurring offers, including clearer eligibility labels.

Explore the case study

The challenge: Promotional titles had been translated individually into English. Similar types of offers could therefore have different names, making the category or eligibility less explicit.

My contribution: I grouped recurring promotions into consistent categories rather than treating each title as an isolated translation. For example, I used “VIP Only” as a clear label for offers restricted to VIP players.

My thinking: A promotional label needs to communicate useful information quickly. Consistent wording gives players a familiar way to identify an offer and gives the team a repeatable approach to naming similar promotions.

The deliverable: A reusable naming approach based on promotion type, with clearer English labels. I do not have usability data measuring its effect on player recognition.
